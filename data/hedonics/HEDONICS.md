### What the Ma 2021 data say [OWN, panel means of 198 pairs]
- Mixture pleasantness lies between its components in 60.6% of pairs; 14.6% below the worse component, 24.7% above the better one.
- Predicting mixture pleasantness (RMSE, r vs observed): plain mean 0.450 / 0.914; min 0.572 / 0.911; max 0.855 / 0.694; intensity-weighted mean 0.419 / 0.927; intensity^2-weighted 0.395 / 0.934. Regression y = -0.86 + 0.82*wI + 0.39*min, R2 0.874 (in-sample).
- Caveat: component pleasantness spans only ~2 to 6.7 (SD ~0.9) with isointensity design, so these r are an optimistic ceiling for wide ranges; split-half reliability of panel means is r = 0.83 (50 random splits of 60 raters).
- Pairs with contrasting component pleasantness (|dP| above median 0.66): mixture sits 0.55 above the worse component and 0.16 below the mean; only 7% fall below both components. Pairs with similar pleasantness: 22% fall below both and 41% above both (partial additivity/emergence, matching Ma 2020's "6 mixtures with partial addition" [SNIPPET]).
- Individual-level: r(PAB, mean(PA,PB)) = 0.71 (per rating row), so individual prediction is much harder than panel-level.
- Mixture intensity: +0.27 above the stronger component, +0.69 above component mean, far below sum (hypoaddition). Mixture pleasantness vs mixture intensity r = -0.47 here (more intense = less pleasant within this isointensity-ish panel).

## 2. Empirical rules, with encoding

R1. **Intensity-weighted averaging (the best-supported mixture rule).** Lapid 2008: "sin" model weighted by component intensity R2 0.8848/0.9406/0.9042/0.8711 on four pairs; the arithmetic mean was worst (R2 as low as 0.1415); the 25:75 mixes sat nearer the more intense component in 3 of 4 cases [OPENED]. Ma 2020: component pleasantness + tau intensity weights, panel R2 > 0.80, RMSE < 0.67 for contrasting pairs; individual level hard [SNIPPET]. My replication: above. Encode: P_mix = sum w_i P_i with w_i = softmax(k * perceived intensity_i) or I_i^2/sum(I^2); learn k. Needs a component perceived-intensity model (concentration x potency; Ravia/Snitz intensity data).
R2. **Mixture pleasantness is usually between components; emergence only when components are alike in pleasantness.** Lapid: "fell between" in most cases, hyperadditivity at 50:50 for carvone/linalool [OPENED]; Ma 2020: 6 pairs with partial addition [SNIPPET]; [OWN] 60.6% between, and out-of-range cases concentrated in similar-pleasantness pairs. Encode: add a residual term u(pair) with variance increasing as 1/(|P_A-P_B|+eps), or an interaction feature "similarity of component pleasantness" x "structural/odor-character overlap".
R3. **Negativity dominance (unpleasant component pulls harder than a symmetric average).** Rozin & Royzman 2001: negative entities weigh more, steeper negative gradients [SNIPPET abstract]. Lapid reports no general unpleasant-dominance claim; the butanoic/phenylethyl 50:50 mix near the unpleasant component is the closest case [OPENED]. [OWN]: adding a min term to wI gives beta_min = 0.39 beyond weighted mean (in-sample), but the min-only predictor is worse than the mean (RMSE 0.57 vs 0.45) and the mean-offset in contrasting pairs is only -0.16: dominance is mild in this panel, whose components are mostly mid-pleasantness. Encode: asymmetric loss/weight, w_i *= (1+gamma*1[P_i < 0]), gamma prior ~0 to 0.5, fit on data containing strongly unpleasant components (Lapid-style acids), which the Ma panel lacks.
R4. **Pleasantness is the main axis of odor perception and is largely predicted by molecule identity/properties; culture matters little.** Arshamian 2022: culture 6% of variance in pleasantness rankings, individual taste 54%, molecular identity 41%, 225 people (EurekAlert says 235; unresolved) in 9 non-Western cultures [SNIPPET abstract]. Khan 2007: first PCA axis of perception = pleasantness, matched by first physicochemical axis [SNIPPET]; r = -0.61 for PC1 vs rating in a later addendum [SNIPPET]. Encode: use a Keller-trained single-molecule hedonic score as the component feature; use population-level model, with a per-person random effect holding about half the variance (so cap attainable accuracy for individuals; the 54% is a ceiling-setter).
R5. **Intensity amplifies hedonic strength and familiarity tracks pleasantness for pleasant odors only.** Distel 1999: intensity positively correlated with familiarity and hedonic strength, 18 odorants, women in 3 countries [SNIPPET]; Doty 1975: pleasantness-intensity correlated for 7 of 10 compounds but not a common function of concentration, so the link is odorant-specific [SNIPPET]; Delplanque 2008: pleasantness-familiarity correlation strong for pleasant, not significant for malodors [SNIPPET]. Encode: hedonic term = sign(P_i) * f(I_i) with odorant-specific concentration slope; separate "familiarity" feature interacting with P only when P > 0.
R6. **Odor-taste congruency: odors that smell sweet enhance tasted sweetness and (liking) when the pairing is appropriate.** Stevenson, Prescott, Boakes 1999: degree to which an odor smells sweet was the best predictor of its effect on tasted sweetness; caramel also suppressed sourness [SNIPPET]; learned via co-exposure ("learned synesthesia") [SNIPPET]; a 2021 exposure-protocol study found no associative-learning effect [SNIPPET], so effect is not universal. Schifferstein & Verlegh 1996: odor-taste mixture pleasantness correlates positively with congruency, but congruency/pleasantness ratings cannot replace similarity ratings for predicting taste enhancement [SNIPPET, secondary]. I did NOT obtain effect sizes. Encode: for odor+taste/flavor, add feature congruency = learned association strength (odor-descriptor "sweet/fruity/savory" x taste), initialised from FlavorDB/GoodScents descriptors and recipe co-occurrence (section 3).
R7. **Complexity: more structurally complex single molecules evoke more notes and are rated as more pleasant; low-complexity molecules are aversive.** Kermen 2011: number of notes vs complexity log-fit; low complexity rated more unpleasant; no effect on trigeminal notes [SNIPPET]; Keller 2016 also found complexity and size correlate with pleasantness and sulfur compounds unpleasant [SNIPPET]. Mixture-level analogue (unverified here): Berlyne's inverted U, hedonic peak at moderate novelty/complexity [SNIPPET, from a beer study that found the inverse U for novelty but linear for familiarity and complexity]. Encode: quadratic term in component count / novelty with prior mode at moderate values; for single components, add molecular-complexity feature. Weiss: olfactory white (40-component) is mid-pleasant 0.46 [OPENED], suggesting many-component mixtures regress to a neutral mean: shrink predicted P toward ~0.46 (0-1 scale) as component count grows past ~30.
R8. **Exposure/neophobia (not verified).** Mere exposure raises liking of novel stimuli, with ambiguity about direction of causation (Sluckin et al. via snippet); neophobia lowers liking of unfamiliar foods [SNIPPET]. I did not open Pliner 1982 or Zajonc, so no effect size. Encode: person-level covariate (neophobia score) multiplying novelty penalty.

Not found / not obtained: effect sizes for concentration-pleasantness inverted U curves of unpleasant odors beyond Doty's qualitative result; Cain/Moskowitz; Zajonc; Pliner; Stevenson effect magnitudes. Also unverified: Arshamian's exact N.

## 3. Recipe / food-pairing proxies (weak population-level liking)

- **Ahn et al. 2011 flavor network (Sci Rep 1:196).** DOWNLOADED to `raw/ahn_2011/` from the journal's SI (static-content.springer.com; the PMC mirror returned an HTML challenge): `x3/srep00196-s3.csv` = 56,498 recipes (ingredient lists by cuisine, e.g. "African,chicken,cinnamon,...") and `x2/srep00196-s2.csv` = 221,781-line ingredient-pair table (counts of shared compounds, e.g. "comte_cheese,grape,57"). Licence: article is open access in Sci Rep; I did not verify the data licence, treat as research-use. Finding: Western cuisines pair ingredients sharing more compounds, East Asian/S. European avoid sharing [SNIPPET]; so shared-compound count is a cuisine-conditional, not universal, liking proxy.
- **FlavorDB (Garg 2018, NAR 46:D1210).** 25,595 flavor molecules; 2,254 linked to 936 natural entities [SNIPPET]. Pyrfume `flavordb/` holds scraped odor/flavor percepts per CID; its manifest cites CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 [OPENED]. Site: cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/flavordb. Not downloaded (not needed: scrape, NC licence).
- **RecipeDB (Batra 2020, Database baaa077).** >118,000 recipes [SNIPPET]; site cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/recipedb; I could not find a download or licence on the page; CulinaryDB page offers a data zip under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 [OPENED]. Treat RecipeDB as request-only; not downloaded.
- Use: recipe co-occurrence (PMI of ingredient pairs/molecule sets) as a prior for "pairing acceptability", NOT hedonic magnitude; popularity confounds (ingredient frequency, cuisine). Validate the proxy against Ma-type pair pleasantness before using it; no such validation exists in what I opened.

## 4. Recommendation
1. Train/validate the mixture head on Ma 2021 (6,660 rows) with component-level inputs (single-molecule P from Keller, intensities from concentration models); baseline to beat: intensity^2-weighted mean RMSE 0.395 on panel means.
2. Request raw data from Lapid/Sobel (strong unpleasant components, ratio sweeps) and Ma/Thomas-Danguin follow-ups; the Ma panel lacks strongly aversive components, so R3 is untestable on open data.
3. Ravia/Snitz/Bushdid supply perceptual-distance structure only; use for component similarity feature, not hedonics.
4. A genuinely new tasting dataset (ternary+ mixtures, with liking) remains the missing piece.
